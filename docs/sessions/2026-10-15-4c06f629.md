### Claude · 05:34

I think it's a good idea. Logs are where problems go to be forgotten: nobody in the back office reads them, and on a single-tenant deployment there may be no one watching them at all. Incidents turn "something went wrong" into a to-do someone owns and closes.

Your model works as the core. I'd add a few things:

- **Source and link:** a `kind` (pdf, email, payment, …) and an optional link to the affected order, offer or conversation. Then staff can jump straight there, and "customer" is often implied by the link.
- **Who resolved it and when:** `resolved_at` and `resolved_by`, so the history shows who closed what.
- **Technical details apart from the description:** the description is readable for staff ("The order confirmation PDF could not be created"). The raw error goes in a collapsible field for whoever fixes the cause.
- **No floods:** if the same kind for the same order is already open, count it up ("occurred 3 times, last at …") instead of creating new rows.
- **Visible without looking for it:** a badge with the number of open incidents in the admin sidebar.

Where incidents would come from:
